Plain

Everything that follows is plain.
One might wonder why anyone would call it a culture.

Yet competitive advantage tends to come from accumulating plain work faster and more massively than others (our value statement).

To do that, one has to keep doing it consciously until it becomes second nature.
And when an organisation drifts, it needs words that bring it back.

So we have written down what plain means to us, and called it our culture.

BizDev Tribe, WTP Is King, Demand Now, Customer Touch, Rejection Is Joy, Grand Entrance, Grand Refusal, Self Version Control, The World Makes No Sense, Flip Every Card, The Genetic Bat, Plain Stairs, and finally, Business is a Game, Companies are Clubs.

We will take them in order.

BizDev Tribe

We are a tribe of people who love developing businesses.

"What is a business?" "How does a business succeed?"
These are our daily bread.

So what, to us, is the essence of a business?

WTP Is King

A business is customers paying money.
If they do not pay, it is not a business.

Social purpose and WTP (willingness to pay) are not unrelated, but correlate much less than one might think.

Some things with a purpose do not sell.
Some things without one sell anyway.

So what we watch is WTP.
When do people open their wallets, and when do they keep them shut?

That is all there is.

And there is timing.

Demand Now

Money arrives now, or some day.
Demand now, demand later.

A business lives on demand now and saves the surplus for demand later.
That order is fixed.

Startups tend to go after demand five years from now.
That demand, however, does not pay this month’s salaries.
A company forever looking five years ahead goes bankrupt in the meantime.

So how does one tell what the demand is now?

Not by thinking.
If thinking worked, nobody would struggle.

Customer Touch

The only way to find out demand now is to ask.
Say to someone, "We give you this. Give us money in exchange."

If they say no, there was no demand now.
If yes, there was.

We call this customer touch.
We say, "Go touch customers."

Very simple, yet people tend to avoid doing it.
Why?
They are afraid of being hurt by a No.

So we need a philosophy to deal with this.

Rejection Is Joy

If being hurt is the worry, the fix is simple.

Enjoy rejections.

Not "try not to mind it."
Not minding is just grinning and bearing it, and nobody can grin for long.
If it is not fun, it does not last.

The BizDev Tribe has fun with it, because a rejection is new information.

"Interesting. At this time, put this way, this person says no."
Then what would get a yes? And what about someone else?
Ideas for improvement keep popping up. Curiosity does the rest.

"No" is a treasure trove.
It is a wonderful answer that makes the heart leap.

Grand Entrance

When rejection is joy, there is nowhere left to fear.
We can walk head-on into the places ordinary people are too afraid to sell to.

Ninety-nine refusals, ninety-nine joys.
One sale, more joy still.
Joy either way, so why not go?

No tricks needed.
"Interested?" "No." "I see. Thank you very much."
That is that.

Most people do not mind if someone comes at them head-on, without tricks.
Take the no with a smile, and people will remember you.
"I turned them down, yet they were nice."

Rejection is opportunity.
No need to tiptoe.

Wherever we go, we make the Grand Entrance.

And it works the other way round, too.

Grand Refusal

As we touch customers fast and massively, the business speeds up.
Naturally, we get touched a lot ourselves.

If we fretted each time about being disliked for saying no, we would slow down.

So we set our acceptance line in advance and publish it.
Outside the line, we always say no. No agonising, no hard feelings.

The unexpected and the borderline?
We turn them down head-on, without tricks.

Decline gracefully, and people will remember you.
"They turned us down, but they were nice."

Rejection is opportunity.
No need to tiptoe.

Wherever we are, we make the Grand Refusal.

That said, some people agree with all of this, and still cannot do it.

What then?

Self Version Control

When people cannot act, it is usually because of how they see themselves.

Why do people hesitate over a customer touch?
Why does rejection not feel like joy?
Why can they not make the Grand Entrance, or the Grand Refusal?

Because each of these moments shows them what others think of them.
When that differs from what they think of themselves, it hurts.

So what is to be done?

We do not lower our self-image.
With a low one, we would simply fear being rated even lower.

Nor do we throw it away.
Without a self-image, we have no way to improve.

Instead, we keep ourselves fluid.

We learn from every rejection, and improve.
The one who was rejected is yesterday’s version, not us.

As a result, we always come out unscathed, in mint condition.

We call this self version control.

Every rejection makes us stronger.

Rejection is joy indeed.

By the way, there is one more thing that stops people from acting.

The World Makes No Sense

People who try to understand the world grow afraid of not understanding it.
They want to understand before they act.
So they research, research some more, and never act.

Because the world does not really make sense.

Humanity is extraordinarily varied.
That variety, through the division of labour and the market economy, lets us all grow richer.

But for the same reason, as we are very different from each other, no one can grasp the WTP as a whole.

None of this dismisses trying to understand customers.
But anyone who waits to understand before acting will never act.

So we take it as given that the world makes no sense, and get on with acting.

We get on with customer touches, and read what comes back as an oracle.

"Heaven knows why, but it seems the customer (i.e. God) desires this, for now."

We take it, dimly, as demand now, update our self version, and carry on.

Guided by the 1% we understand about the world and ourselves, we take on the 99% we do not.

How do we do it?

Flip Every Card

A hundred cards lie face down. One of them wins. Nobody knows which.
How does one find the winner?

Our answer:
Flip every card, at full speed.

That there is a winning card is the 1% we understand.
Where it lies is the 99% we do not.
The number of attempts, then, is our weapon against the 99% we do not understand.

Deep thinking risks slowing the attempts down.
So we accumulate plain work superfast and massively (our value statement).

Not "execute superfast and massively", but "accumulate".
That matters.

Unless our actions pile up in one direction, we go nowhere.
The 1% we understand sets that direction.

We step up to the plate as often as we can, and swing as hard as we can.
But without a bat, there are no hits, let alone home runs.

So how do we choose the bat?

The Genetic Bat

The bat we carry to the plate has to last.
If we changed bats every time the world changed, nothing would ever accumulate.

What does not change, even when the world does?

Ourselves.

"But didn’t you say we keep changing, through self version control?"

We did.

But version control is not wandering off in every direction.
Our versions build up in a set direction.

That direction is the bat.

So what gives us a tendency that never changes?

Our genes.

As one may have noticed, some of us are rather better at certain things than others.
Training changes some of that, but building on strengths tends to pay better than patching weaknesses.

So we choose the bat by what we do better than others.

The same goes for a group.

Put people where their strengths lie, and the group gains a genetic bat of its own.
Capabilities, in management-theory jargon.

So we take the genetic bat, the 1% we understand about ourselves, and swing it at the 99% we do not understand.

So how, exactly, do we swing it?

Plain Stairs

Knowing one’s strengths (the genetic bat) is no guarantee.
Plenty of people and organisations still give up.

What goes wrong?

Well, most leap straight at a hard goal, crash, lose heart and stop.

To avoid that, we carve the way to the goal into steps.
Each step is a plain thing, which plainly gets done, by plainly doing it.

We call these the plain stairs.

WTP sets where we are going (ToBe).
The genetic bat tells us where we are (AsIs).
Plain stairs fill the gap between them.

Then we race up the plain steps, one after another, superfast and massively.
That is what "Accumulate plain work superfast and massively" (our value statement) means in practice.

And while we practise it ourselves, we want to bring it to the world, too.

Why?

Because we believe (at the risk of raising eyebrows) that

"Business is a Game, Companies are Clubs."

Business is a Game, Companies are Clubs

The historian Johan Huizinga called humankind Homo Ludens: the playing human.
In his view, culture was born out of play, in the form of play.

We agree.

Civilisation and technology, as we see them, are by-products: things that turned up while we were busy playing.

In the "WTP Is King" section, we wrote that social purpose and WTP "correlate much less than one might think".

Some industries that civilisation cannot do without run on thin margins.
Others, which (with respect) fewer people would miss, make a fortune.

Business success and social purpose are, to a large extent, different things.

Besides, put social purpose first and one’s thinking hits a wall.

A business provides a service in exchange for money.
Which means the service is fungible.
And if it pays, there will always be someone doing it. There are eight billion of us, after all.

So what is the point of doing a fungible activity that someone would do anyway?

Our answer is this.

Business is a Game, played to a set of rules.
Companies are Clubs, made up of people we want to win with.

Social purpose seeps out along the way, by chance, in passing, if at all.

Why do we commit to our customers’ success through our businesses, alongside our teammates?

To win together.

So, with the culture set out here, we look forward to winning with you, again and again.
